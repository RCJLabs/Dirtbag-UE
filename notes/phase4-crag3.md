# Crag 3: the Sun Terrace, and what aspect actually does

**2026-08-21.** A third crag should answer a question the first two don't.
Roadside is east-facing, the Cave north-facing, so the obvious move was to
pick a remaining aspect and write a story around it. I measured first, and
the measurement said something more interesting than the story would have.

## Aspect does almost nothing in summer

All four aspects, one year, seed `gym-1`:

**High summer (days 170–230)**

| aspect | days | peak friction | window | peak hour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North | 25 | 0.880 | 1.34h | 5.5 |
| East | 25 | 0.871 | 1.01h | 5.3 |
| South | 25 | 0.880 | 1.34h | 5.5 |
| West | 25 | 0.880 | 1.34h | 5.5 |

**North, South and West are identical.** Not similar — identical. In high
summer the prime window lands at about **5:30am**, before the sun is on any
face at all, so which way the rock points cannot matter. Only East differs,
and only slightly, because the sun reaches it first.

### This contradicts the Cave's stated reason for existing

`notes/phase4-cave.md` and the crag's own comment say: *"north-facing is the
whole point of it… in high summer, when the season model puts the window at
dawn and nowhere else, this is the only rock in the valley worth walking
to."*

**The second half of that sentence refutes the first.** The window is at
dawn — which is exactly why north-facing buys nothing there. The reasoning
was written from climbing folklore, which is true about real crags, and
never checked against this model. The Cave is still a good venue; it is the
only rope crag, it costs a belayer, and that is plenty. But it is not the
summer crag, and the note claiming so has been corrected rather than left.

## Aspect does a great deal in winter

**Midwinter (days 1–60)**

| aspect | days | peak friction | window | peak hour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North | 28 | 0.891 | 3.06h | 13.3 |
| East | 36 | 0.887 | 2.44h | 13.6 |
| **South** | **42** | 0.885 | **1.30h** | **13.8** |
| West | 38 | 0.899 | 1.70h | 14.0 |

South gets **half again as many winter days as north**, in a window **less
than half as long**, landing at about **1:45pm**. Fewer, longer windows
against more, sharper ones — a real trade, and the only place in the year
where the aspect dial earns its keep.

## So: the Sun Terrace

South-facing boulders, high and cold. Eleven lines from V3 to V11 and two
projects (V10, V12) — **sparser and harder than Roadside**, because it is
not somewhere you go instead of Roadside, it is somewhere you go in January
or once Roadside has stopped being hard enough.

Bouldering, not rope: winter is bouldering season, and cold rock is why.

### What a job costs there, stated carefully

The tempting claim is "the crag you cannot climb around a job". **It is
false and I nearly shipped it.** First ninety days, salaried nine-to-five:

| | windows | reachable with a job | lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| the Sun Terrace (south) | 63 | 22 (35%) | **41** |
| the Shaded Cave (north) | 48 | 13 (27%) | 35 |

A salaried climber reaches *more* terrace windows than cave windows, both in
count and in proportion. What is true is narrower and still worth having:
**the terrace is where a job costs you the most** — 41 good days lost
against the cave's 35 — because it has the most winter days to lose and they
land in the middle of them.

## What this leaves open

The aspect lever is weak for three quarters of the year, and `approachHours`
is still read by nothing, so a crag cannot yet be differentiated by being
far away either. Between them that is most of what makes one crag feel
unlike another. Worth knowing before a fourth is ever considered: **the
valley currently distinguishes venues by their guidebook and their
discipline, and by very little else.**
