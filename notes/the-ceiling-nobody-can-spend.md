# The ceiling nobody can spend

*2026-08-24*

Chasing why surgery never happened turned up a number that has nothing to do
with surgery: **a salaried career accumulates $609,045 over thirty years.**
The obvious dismissal is that the salaried job is the trap you are meant to
escape, so a career that stays in it for thirty years is playing badly and
its wallet does not count.

That dismissal does not survive measurement.

## What a career can hold, against what it can buy

The most expensive thing in this game is **Home Base at $30,000** — the top
of `DreamDials::cost`, whose own comment calls it *"the end of one
[career]"*. Above it there is nothing.

Thirty years, six seeds:

| policy | peak cash | days climbed | sends | all-round |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| greedy | $474 | 396 | 25.2 | 5.54 |
| saver | $478 | 562 | 23.0 | 6.32 |
| sponsored | $510 | 316 | 25.2 | 5.10 |
| **hoarder** (`savings=60000`) | **$60,378** | **755** | 25.3 | **6.00** |
| salary | **$609,045** | 538 | 24.2 | 4.32 |

**The hoarder is not a degenerate policy.** It climbs 755 days — more than
any other line in the table, nearly twice `greedy` — sends as much as
anybody, and ends with the equal-best all-round grade in the game. It is a
climber who saves, which is a normal way to play, and it can hold **twice
the price of the most expensive purchase that exists.**

And the salaried career is not a non-climber either: 538 days out, 24.2
sends. It climbs plenty; it just climbs *worse* (4.32 all-round, the lowest
here) — which is the trap working exactly as designed on the climbing axis
while nothing at all happens on the money axis. It ends with **twenty times
the game's largest purchase**, unspendable.

## Why the dream prices do not cover it

`DreamDials` says the prices were set honestly: *"Priced off the savings
sweep, not off vibes. A career passes $10,000 comfortably and $25,000 is
where the climbing curve flattens."* That is true, and it is a statement
about **the climbing-first wallet** — the one that peaks at $474 and saves
toward a target.

It is not a statement about a wallet that keeps earning after the climbing
curve has flattened. Nothing in the price list was ever meant to absorb
$600,000, because when those prices were set nothing in the game produced
$600,000.

## What the 2D game does about it

It has **gym ownership**, and `notes/the-2d-audit.md` lists it among the
fifty systems with no code here at all. It is a recorded cut:
`concepts/DIRTBAG.md` §4 defers it past 1.0 on the grounds that *"the 2D
game took years to accrete these; the 3D game earns them the same way"* —
and `concepts/DECISION-comps-are-back.md` re-affirmed that specific cut on
2026-08-23, naming gym ownership as one of the entries the cut list got
**right**.

So this note is not a claim that the cut was wrong. It is the thing that was
missing when the cut was made: **a number.**

The comps reversal turned on an argument of exactly this shape — *a climbing
career in the 2D game has two arcs and the port has one*. The parallel here
would be *the port has a money curve with a ceiling at $30,000 and a wallet
that reaches $600,000*, and the 2D game's answer to that is a business you
buy and run.

**Recorded, not decided.** Reversing a `concepts/` decision is a pivot
rather than a roadmap edit — that convention was set when comps came back —
so this is Evan's call and nothing here has been changed. What has changed
is that the question now has evidence attached to it.

## The cheaper alternatives, for completeness

If the answer is "not gym ownership, not yet", the same gap has smaller
fixes and they are not exclusive:

- **A fourth dream above Home Base.** The cheapest possible change: one row
  in `DreamDials::cost`. It moves the ceiling without adding a system.
- **Make the salaried trap actually trap.** It currently costs climbing
  quality and nothing else. If the money had somewhere to go, staying in
  the job would be a real trade rather than a slow leak.
- **Price the expensive end of an existing system against the big wallet.**
  Surgery at $2,200 is unreachable for the climbing-first career and
  irrelevant to the saver — `notes/what-never-happens.md` has that story.

None of those is a late-game *system*, which is what the 2D game actually
put there. They are ways of not needing one yet.

## Answered

*2026-08-25.* The gap is closed, and it took two passes of gym ownership to
close it. A fully-built gym absorbs **$61,100** of capital — $25,000 for the
building, $8,000 for the equipment ladder, $5,500 for the two hires, and
$22,600 for the five wings — against the hoarder's $60,378, plus wages,
upkeep and about 367 incident bills over thirty years of ownership.

`notes/the-gym-pass-one.md` and `notes/the-gym-pass-two.md` have the
working. The salaried career's $609,045 is untouched by any of it, and that
is a separate question: a wallet twenty times the largest purchase in the
game is not a hole one system can fill.
