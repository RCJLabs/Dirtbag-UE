# The dog

Last named item in Phase 2. **One actor to place.**

## Place the bowl

`Dirtbag Day Spot` → **Kind = `Dog`**, near the van. That is all.

Walk up:

> `Feed it?  (E)  -  there is a stray at the Lot.  $420`

$3 a time. Feed it three or four times and:

> `It follows you back to the van and lies down.`

No prompt, no confirmation, no adoption screen. You fed a stray until it
stopped being one, which is the only ceremony the life provides.

## What it costs and what it gives

- **Food.** About $1.50 a day — roughly an eighth of what the weekly bills
  take. Noticeable, not crushing.
- **Company.** A settled, fed dog is worth a little psyche, and psyche is
  ability in the resolver. Priced like the Lot's people: worth something,
  never worth a grade.
- **Guilt.** A hungry dog is worth *more* in the other direction than a
  happy one is in yours. That is the honest arithmetic of having taken
  something on.

It lands in `ClimberForSession`, the same place fatigue does — so it is in
the body you climb in, not a line of text.

## The van

This is the part worth playing with. A parked van runs about 18F above the
air, and the conditions system already knows the air temperature hour by
hour. Above 78F the van is not somewhere a dog can be left.

A shift is four hours at a desk a dog cannot come to. Measured across 600
days:

| you start your shift at | days the van is too hot |
|---|---|
| **9am** | **0%** |
| noon | 46% |
| 3pm | 66% |
| 6pm | 46% |

**So the dog changes when you work, not whether.** A morning shift is always
fine. An afternoon one, two days in three, means the van hits 84–86F and you
knew it — the game does not stop you, it just says so afterwards:

> `You could hear it from the desk. The van hit 86F.`

and takes it out of your psyche, between a fifth and three quarters of a
grade depending on how hot.

**Where it actually bites:** most days at Roadside the window is late
afternoon, so morning-shift-evening-climb resolves it cleanly. The real
squeeze is a day whose window lands in the *morning* — then climbing when
the rock is good and working when the van is safe are the same hours, and
one of them has to give.

## What to send back

1. **Is the adoption moment any good?** It is deliberately unceremonious.
2. **Does the van rule feel fair or fussy?** It never blocks you. If being
   told off after the fact is annoying rather than affecting, say so.
3. **Is $3 a meal the right weight?** It should be a small ongoing drag you
   notice at the end of a thin week.
4. **Did you name it?** You cannot yet. If you want to, that is a text box
   and the same job as the first-ascent widget — worth doing both at once.
