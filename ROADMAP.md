# ROADMAP — Dirtbag (Unreal reimagining)

Working title **Dirtbag** (3D). Decision log and concept detail live in `concepts/`.
Convention (house style): numbered phases, explicit **Done when** gates, one CURRENT MILESTONE marker. Work only on the current milestone; when its criteria pass, update status, add a changelog line, advance the marker, commit.

**The design in one line:** the 2D game's life-sim and session mechanics ("2D minigames, 3D staging" — the character climbs, the player drives the attempt with HOLD TO CLIMB / hold-to-load / pump-bar verbs, the sim arbitrates), staged in a 3D valley with a gym, crags, the Lot, the van, and the dog.

**Architecture rules (carried from the house method):**
- Sim core is engine-free C++ in `Sim/` — plain structs and pure functions, no Unreal types. The identical translation units compile in the UE module and in the standalone g++ harness (`Sim/run-tests.sh`). The 2D game is the reference implementation and balance lab.
- `Math.random` equivalents are banned; all randomness through the seeded named-stream RNG in `Sim/DirtbagRng.*`.
- Content is data, never code: routes, gear, jobs, events land in DataTables mirrored by plain structs.
- Save shape changes bump a SAVE_VERSION and ship a migration, from the first save onward.

## Phase 0 — Session Proof   ✅ DONE 2026-08-17

The one question this phase answered: **is a minigame-driven session tense and legible on a 3D wall?** — **Yes.** Gates passed on the blockout wall: the stat-block test (same route, two climbers, visibly different attempts, zero animation changes), the tension test (a pumped HOLD TO CLIMB attempt at the limit feels tense), and the watcher test (a V4 and a V7 distinguishable by watching alone). The kill-switch question is retired; from here the risk profile is ordinary game production.

Scope: a blockout gym room (no art pass), third-person walk-up, tap the wall to enter a session. The climber ascends a route spline hold-to-hold using a basic climbing animation set. The pump bar and the HOLD TO CLIMB verb (hold to move, release to shake out) drive per-move resolution through the C++ session sim. Falls and sends both staged: the peel-off, the mat landing, the top-out.

Container-side groundwork (done first, no editor needed): `Sim/` core — RNG, core types, session resolver — with golden-vector tests green in the standalone harness. Editor-side work follows `SETUP.md`.

**Done when:**
1. A watcher can tell how close an attempt was without reading a number (playtest on someone who isn't the dev).
2. A deliberately pumped attempt feels tense through the minigame alone — the 2D verbs survive the transplant.
3. The same route resolves visibly differently for two climber stat blocks with zero animation changes (the sim, not the staging, decides).
4. `Sim/run-tests.sh` green; session resolution is deterministic per seed.

## Changelog

- 2026-08-18 — **Phase 3 opens: gear that wears, and an honest look at the money loop** (`Sim/DirtbagGear.*`, `notes/phase3-economy.md`). Measured first: a year earned $4,860 across 81 shifts against $5,150 of costs, cash never below $93, **22% of days worked**, and no decision all year forced by money — because bills are a flat drip with nothing to do with climbing, so working more always solves them and the two loops never meet. Shoes are the first cost that scales with what you actually do: rubber wears **by the move** and faster the harder you pull, dead rubber costs grades before it costs money (1.1 at zero, squared, worst on edging holds, so a worn pair pushes you onto slopers long before it stops you), and the decision is resole at $55 while the uppers hold or $165 when they will not. Cash now bottoms at $11 — but the notes say plainly that most of that is a probe fix (the player had never eaten once all year, hunger topping out at 30 against a threshold of 45) and shoes are only 6% of the year's spend. What shoes bought is the *shape* of the cost, not its size. The economy still has no lump, which is what van breakdowns are for, and that is the next bite.
- 2026-08-18 — **Freshness is worth something, and the session says where you are.** Skin bit only below 3, only on crimps, and capped at 0.45 grades — so skin 9 and skin 3 were identical to climb on and a season played fresh matched one played wrecked. It is now a curve across the whole range (squared: 9→7 is nearly free, 3→1 decides everything) with holds mattering, so a V5 on V5 crimps runs 51% fresh against 7% on shot tips while jugs only fall 78%→58%. A jug day on wrecked tips is now a real option rather than a free one. Re-measured across three seeds, resting gives **~2.6× the sends** and no seed does worse. Paired with `ReadSession` (*Ready / Cold / SkinThin / Wrecked*), which the wall says when you walk up mid-session — the warmth trap was that a line you cannot start is one you can never warm up on, and nothing said so; a test pins that two or three easy problems clear it. The season now reads like a season: nine lines tried instead of one, first-move odds 52% against 4%, and *Send Train* sitting at 139 burns with a high point of two moves.
- 2026-08-18 — **Played a season headless, and found that flailing was the fastest way to get strong** (`Sim/tools/season.cpp`, `notes/phase2-season-probe.md`). Training read the grade on the tag and never `result.highpoint`, so falling off move one of something impossible trained exactly as well as nearly doing it — a policy that always picked the hardest line reached **V8.7 in a year without a single send**. Targeted skills now scale by how far up you actually got, with a floor for what pulling on teaches anyway; the same year runs V5.0 → V5.9 and a test pins it. Two findings recorded and deliberately not rushed into fixes: **warmth is earned per move, so a line you cannot start is one you can never warm up on** (self-reinforcing, and the game never says so), and **rest days buy almost nothing** because skin is conserved — ~550 burns a year however you spread them. That second one first appeared as a clean 3× improvement on one seed and evaporated across five; it is written up as disproved rather than quietly dropped. A year otherwise runs clean: no drift, no broke days, the Lot takes lines on schedule, and the save round-trips at the end.
- 2026-08-18 — **The dog** (`Sim/DirtbagDog.*`, **SAVE_VERSION 5**), and with it Phase 2's named scope is complete. A stray at the Lot that stops being one after three or four meals — no prompt, no adoption screen, because the life does not provide one. It costs about $1.50 a day, is worth a little psyche when settled and rather more against you when hungry, and lands in `ClimberForSession` beside fatigue so it is in the body you climb in rather than a line of text. The sharp part is the van: it runs ~18F above air, the conditions system already knows the air temperature hour by hour, and above 78F a van is not somewhere to leave a dog. A shift is four hours at a desk it cannot come to — measured over 600 days, a 9am shift is safe every time and a 3pm one is not, two days in three. **So the dog changes when you work, not whether**, and it never blocks you: it tells you afterwards ("You could hear it from the desk. The van hit 86F.") and takes a fifth to three quarters of a grade of psyche. Harness 26,016 → 26,070; v1 through v4 saves all still load.
- 2026-08-18 — **The Lot reaches the player** (`notes/phase2-lot.md`): a Day Spot with **Kind = Fire** — rest with company, naming who is there and what they are on this week — plus **B at any crag wall** to ask for beta, answered by whoever can actually help rather than whoever you know best (Trish is delighted to help and cannot). - 2026-08-18 — **The first-ascent pipeline: clean → work → send → name** (`Sim/DirtbagFirstAscent.*`, **SAVE_VERSION 3**). Phase 2's headline gate, and deliberately not a quest — there is no lock to pick and nothing grants permission. A virgin line is simply filthy, and filthy rock climbs ~4 grades harder (`dirtGradePenalty`) than it will after an afternoon with a brush: a V6 climber sends a clean V5 over half the time and a filthy one under 5%. You may throw yourself at it dirty and fail, which is the honest version of a gate. Cleaning costs ~1.75h to reach workable and composes with the window — brush through the greasy middle of the day, climb when the rock comes into condition. The payoff is what no other route can give: **a project's true grade is hidden and differs from the book's guess** (the three lines here go V7/V7, V8/V7, V9/**V10**), so doing it is the first time anybody knows, and the naming is yours. `NameFirstAscent` never touches `route.name` — the ledger key — so a career's burns survive the renaming. Measured: a V6 climber takes 3 days and 14 burns on the arete but 138 days and 699 on the same-grade traverse (shape beats nominal grade), and the blank wall at V10 refuses even a V9 — the crag keeps one line to grow into. Engine side: `Dirtbag|FirstAscent` nodes, wall prompts leading with the dirt, and project ledgers seeded when the crag loads rather than on first touch — every attempt path invents a *clean* ledger through `MemoryFor` if one is missing, so seeding late would have made the entire mechanic silently absent. Harness 19,613 → 19,813, clean under ASan/UBSan.
- 2026-08-18 — **Roadside Crag: the guidebook as data** (`Sim/DirtbagCrag.*`). 25 named lines and 3 open projects, authored rather than generated — a gym board is a clean V0–V15 ladder that resets, a crag is a fixed book where grades cluster where the rock happened to be climbable, sandbags are famous and specific (*Second Breakfast* has said V2 for years and climbs V3), and some lines have never gone at all. Stars run across the range instead of tracking it, so *Shade Line* V4 is a three-star classic and *Sloper Roulette* V5 is filler. Measured against the ladder before shipping: a V5 climber reads 11 warmups, 4 comfortable, **7 at their limit**, 3 projects and 3 refusals — a day with shape — while a V7 has exhausted the place, which is the pressure to take the projects. `CragLine.displayName` exists from the first commit so naming a first ascent never moves `route.name`, which is the ledger key ProjectMemory and the save file record; renaming a line out from under a career would orphan every burn already put into it, and a test pins it. Engine side: mirrors, `Dirtbag|Crag` nodes, and one board index serving two venues, so moving a wall from gym to crag is a flag rather than a rebuild. The crag owns its own aspect and sets it on load — a second copy is how rock ends up climbing in one aspect's shade with its window computed for another. Harness 19,111 → 19,613 checks.
- 2026-08-18 — **Conditions and the prime window** (`Sim/DirtbagConditions.*`, `notes/phase2-window.md`). The resolver has eaten a `friction` scalar since Phase 0 and nothing ever produced one; this produces it. Seed + day → weather on its own rng stream; weather + aspect + hour → *rock* temperature, which banks the sun's heat and sheds it on a lag, so prime is the crossing when the rock cools back through ideal — the reason you wait after the shade line passes. Three things were measured before anything was written: `frictionWeight` needed no change (0.6 grades reads as nothing and swings a limit V5 from 39% to 70%); the window's **length** is the whole design (skin allows 8–12 burns, so a window over two hours holds a whole day's skin, waiting becomes free, and a limit project falls 98% of the time to someone who knows nothing about it); and getting the peak off the boundaries took three models before dawn-pinning fell from 50% to 12%. Gate passed and pinned by test: 27% with no recon, 62% with six, 60% once the skin is gone before the window opens. Aspect decides *when* — east-facing is bimodal, west peaks in the morning, south comes good at 6pm, north tracks the air. Harness 7,631 → 19,111 checks. Engine side: mirrors, six `Dirtbag|Conditions` nodes, HUD line, and both attempt paths finally resolving against real friction instead of the 0.5 default. The gym stays indoors and flat on purpose.
