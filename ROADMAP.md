# ROADMAP — Dirtbag (Unreal reimagining)

Working title **Dirtbag** (3D). Decision log and concept detail live in `concepts/`.
Convention (house style): numbered phases, explicit **Done when** gates, one CURRENT MILESTONE marker. Work only on the current milestone; when its criteria pass, update status, add a changelog line, advance the marker, commit.

**The design in one line:** the 2D game's life-sim and session mechanics ("2D minigames, 3D staging" — the character climbs, the player drives the attempt with HOLD TO CLIMB / hold-to-load / pump-bar verbs, the sim arbitrates), staged in a 3D valley with a gym, crags, the Lot, the van, and the dog.

**Architecture rules (carried from the house method):**
- Sim core is engine-free C++ in `Sim/` — plain structs and pure functions, no Unreal types. The identical translation units compile in the UE module and in the standalone g++ harness (`Sim/run-tests.sh`). The 2D game is the reference implementation and balance lab.
- `Math.random` equivalents are banned; all randomness through the seeded named-stream RNG in `Sim/DirtbagRng.*`.
- Content is data, never code: routes, gear, jobs, events land in DataTables mirrored by plain structs.
- Save shape changes bump a SAVE_VERSION and ship a migration, from the first save onward.

## Phase 0 — Session Proof   ✅ DONE 2026-08-17

The one question this phase answered: **is a minigame-driven session tense and legible on a 3D wall?** — **Yes.** Gates passed on the blockout wall: the stat-block test (same route, two climbers, visibly different attempts, zero animation changes), the tension test (a pumped HOLD TO CLIMB attempt at the limit feels tense), and the watcher test (a V4 and a V7 distinguishable by watching alone). The kill-switch question is retired; from here the risk profile is ordinary game production.

Scope: a blockout gym room (no art pass), third-person walk-up, tap the wall to enter a session. The climber ascends a route spline hold-to-hold using a basic climbing animation set. The pump bar and the HOLD TO CLIMB verb (hold to move, release to shake out) drive per-move resolution through the C++ session sim. Falls and sends both staged: the peel-off, the mat landing, the top-out.

Container-side groundwork (done first, no editor needed): `Sim/` core — RNG, core types, session resolver — with golden-vector tests green in the standalone harness. Editor-side work follows `SETUP.md`.

**Done when:**
1. A watcher can tell how close an attempt was without reading a number (playtest on someone who isn't the dev).
2. A deliberately pumped attempt feels tense through the minigame alone — the 2D verbs survive the transplant.
3. The same route resolves visibly differently for two climber stat blocks with zero animation changes (the sim, not the staging, decides).
4. `Sim/run-tests.sh` green; session resolution is deterministic per seed.

- 2026-08-19 — **A shut crag would have stayed shut forever.** `FactionDay` — the standing drift and the closure countdown — was called by the season probe and **never by the engine**, so in the actual game access would have been pulled once and never restored for the rest of the save. That is the **third** per-day tick written and left uncalled (`KitDay` bought 365 days of membership for one $75; the body roll was the second), so the rule is now structural rather than remembered: anything that counts down counts down inside `SleepToNextDay`, where a night is. `TestANightIsWhereEverythingCountsDown` fails on the old code and holds all three.
- 2026-08-19 — **Training load and injury: the second body budget** (`Sim/DirtbagBody.*`, `notes/phase4-body.md`, **SAVE_VERSION 11**). Phase 4 work taken out of order and deliberately: both Phase 3 measurements ended at the same wall, that skin caps the year so nothing money buys can add climbing to a season. Load is the second budget — **hard climbing accrues it, it heals over a month rather than six nights, and past 62 it is how you get hurt.** Twelve seasons per policy: an **outdoors-only climber cannot overtrain at all — 0 injuries in twelve seasons**, because 157 washed-out days and 75 rest days mean they climb one day in three. That is not a soft dial, it is a true thing about weather-limited crags. **A hangboard removes that protection**, being the one thing in the game that loads tendons without the weather getting a say. And the warning is legible before it bites: a player who stops training at "everything aches; this is the warning" sends **38 against 24**, and spends **55 days hurt against 1,467**. Injuries are specific and decide what you can still climb on — a pulley ends crimping and leaves slopers alone, a shoulder is the reverse, a lumbrical is pockets and only pockets, an elbow is everything a little and so nobody rests it properly. Nobody gets hurt out of the blue (below the threshold the chance is flat zero, never small), tendons do not tear in a camp chair (the roll only happens on a day you pulled on), and climbing on it is a gamble both ways. **Physio is money for time**, once a week at most — the second thing money buys that hands climbing back rather than moving it around, and the first one a player will actually reach.
- 2026-08-19 — **Four defects the body measurement caught.** The **hangboard accrued no load at all** — the most injury-causing activity in climbing was free, and priced in *skin*, which was never what a board costs; that stand-in existed only because no tendon budget did (skin 1.6→0.3, load 0→14). Load was **asking the wrong question**, measuring grades-over-you, so a player projecting at their limit was over by zero and a season peaked at 6 out of 100 — climbing at your limit *is* maximal intensity. **Aggravation ran away**, turning one bad fortnight into a 226-day injury; severity rises now and days are recomputed from it, so the worst an injury can be is the worst an injury is. And the probe's table had **silently empty columns twice** — a field appended to a printf's values and not its format — so every machine-readable line now carries a `HEAD` row emitted from the same place. Two signatures also became stricter on purpose: `SleepToNextDay` and `ApplyAttemptToDay` take the world RNG as a **required** parameter, because those are the two places the body rolls and a caller who forgot would silently play a game where nobody ever got hurt.
- 2026-08-19 — **The kit: what money should buy, measured** (`Sim/DirtbagKit.*`, `notes/phase3-kit.md`, **SAVE_VERSION 10**). The gate failed because there was nothing to want, so: a second **crash pad** ($260), a **hangboard** ($85), and a **gym membership** ($75/month, the only climbing that ignores the weather). Twelve seasons per policy. Buying all three is **neutral** — 43 sends against a non-owner's 38, inside a per-seed variance of 0–18 — but forcing the second pad gives **59, at the same crag days and the same burns**. That gap is the finding, and it generalises: **skin caps the year, so kit that spends skin only moves climbing around; only kit that raises the value of skin already spent can add to a season.** The pad costs no skin and makes every burn you were taking anyway more likely to land; the board and the gym both draw on the budget the crag draws on, which is exactly why `kitted` climbs 361 fewer days outside and comes out level. The board is not a trap but using it badly is: fired on any dead day it gave **0 sends across five seasons** against a non-owner's 12, and gated on genuinely surplus skin the same item at the same price gave **24 against 12** — same object, opposite sign, decided entirely by when you reach for it. And the item measured to matter is the one the probe **never once afforded** in twelve seasons: $260 against a year whose cash high is $240. That is the first time in this project that the thing worth having is the thing you cannot quite reach. Gate still not passed, but the shape of the answer is known rather than guessed.
- 2026-08-19 — **Three defects the kit measurement caught.** `KitDay` existed and was called by nobody, so a single $75 bought 365 days of membership — it ticks down in `SleepToNextDay` now, with everything else that runs out. The hangboard **out-trained the wall four to one** (1.44 finger points a day against climbing's 0.32) because nothing capped sessions and skin alone allowed eleven; now one a day at 40% of a light session. And pads defaulted to zero, which silently applied a penalty nobody could afford to remove and **took the five-seed send count from 17 to 3** — that is not a new decision for the player, it is the game quietly getting harder, so everybody starts with one pad and the second is the purchase.
- 2026-08-19 — **The Phase 3 gate, measured — and it fails, for a reason worth having** (`notes/phase3-economy.md`, **SAVE_VERSION 9**). The probe could never answer "does the money pressure the climbing" because every policy in it works whenever cash runs low; a thermostat cannot tell you how cold it is outside. So the measurement is a control: a **`kept` player with no bills, free food and free fuel**, who never works a day. Across five seeds their season is *indistinguishable* — 133 climbing days against 135, 524 burns against 522, and **fewer** sends than the player paying rent. Seventy days of work a year costs nothing. Three reasons, in order: an odd job is four hours starting at seven and the window is later, so a shift costs you the morning and the morning was never the climbing; **skin caps the year, not time** (133 climbing days out of the 208 that have a window at all, leaving 232 days to put work in); and the real one — **there is nothing to want**. The only discretionary spending in a whole season is shoes, $275 against $6,601 earned, and the year ends with $133 in the bank and nowhere for it to go. Money cannot pressure climbing because money does not *buy* climbing. Same root cause kills the faction choice: greedy and careful end the year on the same cash, so turning down the guidebook money is free and therefore not a decision. Recorded as **honestly unmet**, with the next mechanic named rather than a dial pass proposed: things worth money that make you a better climber.
- 2026-08-19 — **Seasonal daylight, and fuel** (`DaylightHours`, `FirstLightHour`, `LastLightHour`, `FuelFor`). `firstLight`/`lastLight` were pinned at 6-to-20 all year, so a nine-to-five always left three hours of evening and a salaried season climbed **as many burns as an unemployed one — 521 against 518 — for $19,900 more**. Light now swings around solar noon with the solstice leading the warmest day by thirty: **16.5 hours midsummer, 7.9 midwinter, dark at 16:26 in December** — before you clock off. And driving stopped being free: at $9 an hour (a van at eighteen to the gallon, close to the real arithmetic) a season spends $918–$1,224 getting to the crag, which is **the only cost in the game that goes up the more you climb**. Days worked moved 15% → 19%. Two probe bugs fell out: it climbed by headlamp until the skin ran out, and it drove to locked gates — a hundred pointless hours a year, free until fuel had a price.

- 2026-08-18 — **Factions v1, and the town as data** (`Sim/DirtbagFactions.*`, `Sim/DirtbagTown.*`, **SAVE_VERSION 8**). Four groups on **two opposed axes** — Old Guard ↔ Scene, Development ↔ Stewardship — so pleasing one costs another and there is no maxing everything. Standing attaches to things the player was doing anyway rather than to a menu: **brushing a project** pleases the development crew and annoys the stewards, **a first ascent** credits both ends of an axis only if the style was good, and on the odd-jobs board **the best-paying gig is the one that costs you most** (shooting guidebook photos, $130, against the old guard and the stewards both) while trail work buys it back. - 2026-08-18 — **Seasons: the window migrates, and a job costs you the winter** (`SeasonalCentreF`, `SeasonName`). One `baseTempF` covered the whole year, so windows sat in the same part of the day forever — which is why a nine-to-five and an east-facing crag never conflicted. The year now swings 20°F either side of 48: **summer centres on 68°F where only dawn is cool enough and just 33% of days are worth walking to the crag for; winter centres on 28°F where the good hours are 14:12** — the middle of the working day. Measured, **44% of winter windows are unreachable on a workday** against 0% in summer, and across a year the salaried climber arrives too late for about twice as many windows (60 vs 34, 48 vs 26, 45 vs 22). Written up with its limits: sends fall (7→2 on one seed) but not catastrophically, because weekends and the shoulder seasons hand most of the year back, and money is still solved completely. The trap is real, moderate, and seasonal. One probe bug found on the way: it was climbing in the window's *peak* conditions even when the clock was hours past the window, which hid the entire effect.
- 2026-08-18 — **The odd-jobs board and the salaried job — and the trap is not a trap yet** (`Sim/DirtbagJobs.*`, `notes/phase3-jobs.md`). Ten authored gigs, three a day, pay wobbling ±15%, and the coupling worth having: **some need the van**, so a breakdown costs the repair *and* the work that would have paid for it. Odd-job pay goes to debt first like any other money. The salaried job solves money exactly as designed — a salaried year ends on **$19,669** against the dirtbag's **$145** — and then **fails to cost anything**: the salaried climber climbs the *same number of days*, because work ends at 17:00 and Roadside Crag is east-facing, so its window is the evening. A nine-to-five and an east-facing crag simply do not conflict. Raising the energy cost gives 3/4/4 sends on one seed and 6/2/2 on another, which is noise, so the dial is back where it started rather than tuned until a number looked right. Written up as measured: the trap needs conditions that *move* — aspect (a west-facing crag would be gutted by a nine-to-five), seasons (one `baseTempF` keeps windows in the same part of the day forever), or a reason spontaneity pays. Seasons are the cheapest fix and the one to do next.
- 2026-08-18 — **Phase 3 reaches the player, and bills you cannot pay become debt** (**SAVE_VERSION 7**, `notes/phase3-placement.md`). Cash used to go negative and sit there with nothing behind it; it now floors at zero, the shortfall waits as `owed`, and the next wage clears it before reaching your pocket — a hole with something in it at last. Engine side: mirrors for shoes and all six van parts, `Dirtbag|Gear` and `Dirtbag|Van` nodes, and **every Travel spot now actually drives** — hours on the clock, hours on the van, and the chance that the part you have been ignoring picks this morning. A broken van refuses to go anywhere, which is the only "no" in the game, and the way out is four hours with a spanner rather than money. Two spots to place: **Kind = Van** (does the best repair you can afford — replace, patch, or bodge) and **Kind = GearShop** (resole while the uppers hold, $165 when they will not). The HUD carries what you owe and what is falling apart.
- 2026-08-18 — **The van: wear, breakdowns, and the lump the economy was missing** (`Sim/DirtbagVan.*`, **SAVE_VERSION 6**). Six parts wearing by the hour driven, so the crag you drive to costs more than the one you walk to. Nothing fails without warning — a part must be past its life first, and the van says so (*"the belt is on borrowed time"*). Three ways out: **bodge free for four hours, patch for cash twice, replace properly** — and bodging is always available, which is load-bearing: a stranded player with no money can always spend a morning under the van, so **breakdowns take your season and never your save** (pinned by test). The radiator wears 2.5× faster on warm days, so hot afternoons are both when you cannot climb and when the drive home is a gamble. Tuning took three passes: the first numbers gave 16 breakdowns a year (a running gag, not a van), 4.5× longer lives gave 1 (too rare to notice), and the settled ones give 2–3 in year one. Then it **aged on its own** — across three years the rate climbs to ~9/year because a patch returns only 60% and parts take two before needing replacement, with nearly half of all later repairs bodged because the money was not there. Cash bottoms at $0 with a day too broke to eat. That is the shape the gate wanted: not a bigger drip, but a bill on a morning you had plans.
- 2026-08-18 — **Phase 3 opens: gear that wears, and an honest look at the money loop** (`Sim/DirtbagGear.*`, `notes/phase3-economy.md`). Measured first: a year earned $4,860 across 81 shifts against $5,150 of costs, cash never below $93, **22% of days worked**, and no decision all year forced by money — because bills are a flat drip with nothing to do with climbing, so working more always solves them and the two loops never meet. Shoes are the first cost that scales with what you actually do: rubber wears **by the move** and faster the harder you pull, dead rubber costs grades before it costs money (1.1 at zero, squared, worst on edging holds, so a worn pair pushes you onto slopers long before it stops you), and the decision is resole at $55 while the uppers hold or $165 when they will not. Cash now bottoms at $11 — but the notes say plainly that most of that is a probe fix (the player had never eaten once all year, hunger topping out at 30 against a threshold of 45) and shoes are only 6% of the year's spend. What shoes bought is the *shape* of the cost, not its size. The economy still has no lump, which is what van breakdowns are for, and that is the next bite.
- 2026-08-18 — **Freshness is worth something, and the session says where you are.** Skin bit only below 3, only on crimps, and capped at 0.45 grades — so skin 9 and skin 3 were identical to climb on and a season played fresh matched one played wrecked. It is now a curve across the whole range (squared: 9→7 is nearly free, 3→1 decides everything) with holds mattering, so a V5 on V5 crimps runs 51% fresh against 7% on shot tips while jugs only fall 78%→58%. A jug day on wrecked tips is now a real option rather than a free one. Re-measured across three seeds, resting gives **~2.6× the sends** and no seed does worse. Paired with `ReadSession` (*Ready / Cold / SkinThin / Wrecked*), which the wall says when you walk up mid-session — the warmth trap was that a line you cannot start is one you can never warm up on, and nothing said so; a test pins that two or three easy problems clear it. The season now reads like a season: nine lines tried instead of one, first-move odds 52% against 4%, and *Send Train* sitting at 139 burns with a high point of two moves.
- 2026-08-18 — **Played a season headless, and found that flailing was the fastest way to get strong** (`Sim/tools/season.cpp`, `notes/phase2-season-probe.md`). Training read the grade on the tag and never `result.highpoint`, so falling off move one of something impossible trained exactly as well as nearly doing it — a policy that always picked the hardest line reached **V8.7 in a year without a single send**. Targeted skills now scale by how far up you actually got, with a floor for what pulling on teaches anyway; the same year runs V5.0 → V5.9 and a test pins it. Two findings recorded and deliberately not rushed into fixes: **warmth is earned per move, so a line you cannot start is one you can never warm up on** (self-reinforcing, and the game never says so), and **rest days buy almost nothing** because skin is conserved — ~550 burns a year however you spread them. That second one first appeared as a clean 3× improvement on one seed and evaporated across five; it is written up as disproved rather than quietly dropped. A year otherwise runs clean: no drift, no broke days, the Lot takes lines on schedule, and the save round-trips at the end.

- 2026-08-18 — **The first-ascent pipeline: clean → work → send → name** (`Sim/DirtbagFirstAscent.*`, **SAVE_VERSION 3**). Phase 2's headline gate, and deliberately not a quest — there is no lock to pick and nothing grants permission. A virgin line is simply filthy, and filthy rock climbs ~4 grades harder (`dirtGradePenalty`) than it will after an afternoon with a brush: a V6 climber sends a clean V5 over half the time and a filthy one under 5%. You may throw yourself at it dirty and fail, which is the honest version of a gate. Cleaning costs ~1.75h to reach workable and composes with the window — brush through the greasy middle of the day, climb when the rock comes into condition. The payoff is what no other route can give: **a project's true grade is hidden and differs from the book's guess** (the three lines here go V7/V7, V8/V7, V9/**V10**), so doing it is the first time anybody knows, and the naming is yours. `NameFirstAscent` never touches `route.name` — the ledger key — so a career's burns survive the renaming. Measured: a V6 climber takes 3 days and 14 burns on the arete but 138 days and 699 on the same-grade traverse (shape beats nominal grade), and the blank wall at V10 refuses even a V9 — the crag keeps one line to grow into. Engine side: `Dirtbag|FirstAscent` nodes, wall prompts leading with the dirt, and project ledgers seeded when the crag loads rather than on first touch — every attempt path invents a *clean* ledger through `MemoryFor` if one is missing, so seeding late would have made the entire mechanic silently absent. Harness 19,613 → 19,813, clean under ASan/UBSan.

- 2026-08-18 — **Conditions and the prime window** (`Sim/DirtbagConditions.*`, `notes/phase2-window.md`). The resolver has eaten a `friction` scalar since Phase 0 and nothing ever produced one; this produces it. Seed + day → weather on its own rng stream; weather + aspect + hour → *rock* temperature, which banks the sun's heat and sheds it on a lag, so prime is the crossing when the rock cools back through ideal — the reason you wait after the shade line passes. Three things were measured before anything was written: `frictionWeight` needed no change (0.6 grades reads as nothing and swings a limit V5 from 39% to 70%); the window's **length** is the whole design (skin allows 8–12 burns, so a window over two hours holds a whole day's skin, waiting becomes free, and a limit project falls 98% of the time to someone who knows nothing about it); and getting the peak off the boundaries took three models before dawn-pinning fell from 50% to 12%. Gate passed and pinned by test: 27% with no recon, 62% with six, 60% once the skin is gone before the window opens. Aspect decides *when* — east-facing is bimodal, west peaks in the morning, south comes good at 6pm, north tracks the air. Harness 7,631 → 19,111 checks. Engine side: mirrors, six `Dirtbag|Conditions` nodes, HUD line, and both attempt paths finally resolving against real friction instead of the 0.5 default. The gym stays indoors and flat on purpose.
